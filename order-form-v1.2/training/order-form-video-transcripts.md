# Order Form training videos — scripts & voiceover transcripts

Companion to the **Order Form Academy** page. Three short recordings cover the whole rep journey.
Two screen-capture GIFs of the real production guards are already recorded (silent, for embedding
anywhere): `orderform-renewal-guard.gif` and `orderform-wrong-template-guard.gif`. The voiceover
transcripts below are word-for-word — read them over a Loom/Teams screen recording, or use them
as captions.

---

## Video 1 — "Quote to signed, end to end" (~90 seconds)

**Screen:** a new-business quote in the quote line editor.

> This is the new Order Form, and here's the whole job, start to finish.
>
> I'm building a quote exactly the way I always have — nothing about quoting changes. I've added
> a Lexology Pro product, and because it's a Benefiting Group licence, I'm asked to describe who's
> covered. I'll type: "the Customer's in-house legal function, comprising approximately 25
> individuals." That sentence goes on the contract, word for word — so write it like a contract.
>
> **Screen: Generate Document.** Now I generate the document. I pick the **Subscription Order
> Form** template, keep the default name, and generate. Look at what it built for me: the right
> brand logo for this deal, the legal entity and governing law, the licence wording on every line,
> my name and email as Account Manager, the customer's addresses. I typed one sentence; the
> system did the rest.
>
> **Screen: approved quote.** Once the quote's approved, the draft watermark is gone and this
> button appears: **Send for Signature**. One click. The customer gets an Adobe email, signs on
> any device, and answers two quick questions — do they need a PO on invoices, and their VAT or
> sales-tax number.
>
> **Screen: the quote after signing.** And when they sign? Signed date on the quote. Signed PDF
> filed. Their PO and VAT answers written back. Automatically. You did three things: described
> the group, checked the Primary Contact, clicked send.

## Video 2 — "When it says no" (~60 seconds)

**Screen:** clicking Send for Signature and getting a message. (The two GIFs show the real thing.)

> The send button checks everything before anything leaves the building — and when it says no,
> it tells you exactly why. Let me show you the messages you might meet.
>
> **[GIF: renewal guard]** Here's a renewal quote. I click Send for Signature and it says: "The
> Order Form covers new business for now. Renewals continue on the current process and move to
> the Order Form on 1 January." Nothing was sent, nothing broke — renewals just aren't in scope
> yet.
>
> **[GIF: wrong-template guard]** Here's a quote where the last document was generated with an
> old template. The message names the document, names the template, and tells me the fix: open
> Generate Document, pick Subscription Order Form, generate, try again. Thirty seconds.
>
> You might also see: quote not approved yet — get it approved; we don't know who signs — set the
> Primary Contact on the quote; a Benefiting Group line with no description — fill it in on the
> line. (The signer and the invoice row both default from the Primary Contact; if a different
> person should sign or be invoiced, set the Signatory Contact or Invoice Contact on the quote —
> they sit just under the Primary Contact.)
>
> Every message is the fix. Read it, do it, send again. No ticket needed.

## Video 3 — "The override" (~56 seconds, for managers; includes real line-editor footage)

**Screen:** title slide, then two real production frames of the quote line editor.

> The licence wording on the Order Form is automatic, and it is right almost every time. This
> video is for the exception.
>
> When the wording doesn't match the deal, you have two places to fix it: the Licence Model
> section on the quote line page, or the quote line editor, where you already work. Let me show
> you the editor.
>
> **[QLE frame 1]** This is a real quote in the line editor, on the Subscriptions view. Look to
> the right: License Model Override is just another column, sitting next to the finance code.
>
> **[QLE frame 2]** Click the cell, pick the model that matches the deal — here, Firmwide License
> — and the change lights up orange until you save. Save, regenerate the document, and the
> wording follows.
>
> The override beats everything: the product default and the automatic pricing check, in both
> directions. Use it when the deal is genuinely unusual, and clear it to go back to automatic.
> Ten seconds, no admin, no ticket.

## Recording notes

- Record at 1080p or better, browser zoom 100%; use a sandbox quote or the demo quotes the CRM
  team can stage on request.
- Keep the document name short on the Generate screen (80-character limit — the default is safe).
- The two guard GIFs were captured in production on 28 Aug 2026 and show the live behaviour; they
  can be embedded directly in Teams posts, the Academy page, or slides.
