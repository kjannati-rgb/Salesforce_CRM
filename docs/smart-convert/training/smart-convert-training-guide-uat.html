Smart Convert — Rep Training Guide & UAT Script

Live in production  since 14 August 2026 · UAT completed in FULLUAT · Owner: Kamyar Jannati (Head Data and CRM)

Who this document is for: the RevOps team, to train sales reps and SDRs on the Smart Convert lead-conversion cockpit — with the UAT script retained at the back as the acceptance record and regression pack.

1. What is Smart Convert, and why it exists

Converting a lead used to be where duplicates were born: the same firm created fifteen times (Dentons ×15, KPMG ×11 in production), contacts under the wrong office with the wrong address, and existing customers treated as brand-new prospects.

Smart Convert replaces the native Convert button (now removed from the Lead page) with a guided, 5-step cockpit that:

  • Matches the lead to the right firm automatically using the email domain — no typing, no guessing.
  • Places the contact under the right office (location first — because the office address is stamped onto the contact).
  • Never creates a duplicate person — it finds existing contacts by email and links the enquiry to them.
  • Knows whether the firm already subscribes to the product the enquiry is about (or, when the lead names none, the converting rep's own product line), and frames the convert as New Business, Cross-sell, Upsell or Renewal accordingly.
  • Creates genuinely new firms on the spot — duplicate-checked, in seconds, with the Data Team notified to enrich behind you. No more waiting to sell.
  • Shows the room before you act: the covering account-team member, existing open opportunities, the firm's known contacts.
  • Closes the loop with a one-click follow-up task so no converted lead dies untouched.

3. The wizard — step by step

Step 1 · Inbound

Who arrived: name, title, email, company, location — plus the quality strip (Pardot Grade & Score, email validity, and an ALM Radar fit chip where the lead carries one) which follows you through every screen. Chips appear only when the lead carries that data; a dashed "No Pardot score on this lead" chip means the lead was never scored (usual for manually created leads) — information, not an error. A red "Email: Invalid" chip means verification flagged the address — consider disqualifying instead of converting a dead email.

Step 2 · Match

The engine resolves the email domain to a firm and shows its working in plain language, with a confidence meter:

  • Strong match (one firm owns this domain) — a green "High-confidence match — convert in one click" card appears. The create-opportunity toggle (and currency) sits right on the card, off by default — flip it before clicking if this enquiry should open a deal. Details right? Convert now finishes the whole job in one click. "Review details" walks the full wizard.
  • Several firms share the domain — you pick the right one at the next step; never automatic. Each card shows the firm's domain, its office locations (so similar names separate at a glance) and its footprint.
  • No match — guided create (new firm + office) with duplicate checks first. The new office takes the company name exactly as on the lead (no location suffix — the account name flows onto quotes and contracts as the legal entity name) and the lead's full address, including state.
Free personal domains (gmail, yahoo, etc.) are never used as a firm key — those leads fall back to company-name search and guided create. When the email domain is unknown to us but the lead's website matches a firm we know (alias domains), the match comes through at review confidence — the rep confirms, never one-click.

Step 3 · Choose office

Candidate offices are ranked location first, deal volume second. The recommended office is pre-selected; each card shows city/country, contacts, won opportunities and value.

Why location beats deal volume: the office's address is stamped onto the new contact. An office with more opportunities never outranks the office the person actually works in — opportunity count only breaks ties. When the two disagree, the wizard flags it for review instead of auto-converting.

Wrong firm? "Search & pick a different one" finds any existing firm or office by name (an office hit resolves to its parent firm and pre-selects that office). "Back to the recommendation" undoes the override.

Step 4 · Confirm

  • Account context bar (click to expand): the account team across the firm with the member whose role covers this product highlighted (Law.com CSM / PI CSM / E&I CSM), plus the buying committee — known contacts tagged by role (Knowledge/PSL, Library/Research, L&D, Practice leadership) with gap chips for the personas we're missing.
  • Open opportunities panel: every open opp with stage, type, amount, close date, owner — and a renewal chip with the budget renewal date where relevant. If an open opp already covers this product, join that deal rather than splitting it.
  • Who should this enquiry attach to? An email match pre-selects the existing contact — no second record. A contact at a different office shows a "possible move" note: we attach where they already are and never move people between offices.
  • Create Opportunity toggle — OFF by default. Switch it on only when this enquiry should open a new deal. The currency selector defaults to your user currency — change it if the deal is in another currency.

Step 5 · Done

  • Links to the new Account, Contact and Opportunity, plus a next step tailored to the situation — e.g. "Qualify the GCR enquiry and book a discovery call. Sarah Poole (E&I CSM) covers this firm."
  • One-click follow-up task: Type (Call / Email / Meeting / Product Demo / Product Trial / Connect on LinkedIn / Other), due date, assignee (you, or hand it to the covering CSM), and your comments. The task lands on the new contact and opportunity.

4. Sales motions — what the wizard decides and why

Rules changed 24 September 2026 at Commercial's request (Khris Fenton, VP New Business, Law.com). Before that date, any Closed Won deal at the firm — on any product — made an enquiry a Cross-sell. It no longer does: only active subscriptions relevant to the enquiry count.

Which product is the enquiry about? The product the lead names, if we recognise it. If the lead names nothing (or free text we don't recognise), the converting rep's own product line — the opportunity they create is for what their team sells — and the banner tells the rep so. Leads arriving via Pardot forms have no rep yet, so they use the lead's product only.

Key training message: an existing customer of this product or its family is an opportunity, not a rejection — it's an upsell, renewal or cross-sell. A customer of unrelated products is new business for yours. The only genuine duplicate case is an open opp already covering this brand — then join it, or disqualify as duplicate.

5. Disqualifying — but keeping the record

The Disqualify button never throws the person away. It runs the same convert with no opportunity, links the person to the right account and contact, and records the reason (required). One complete view of every customer — a disqualified lead today is a renewal contact tomorrow.

6. Golden rules (the 30-second version)

  1. Green express card + correct details = one click and you're done.
  2. Location decides the office, not deal counts — the address goes onto the contact.
  3. Never create a second person — if the email matches, link.
  4. Customer of this product or its family ≠ disqualify — it's an upsell / renewal / cross-sell. Customer of something unrelated = new business for you.
  5. Open opp for the same brand? Add to it, don't split the deal. The opportunity toggle is off by default — opt in when a new deal is genuinely starting.
  6. Check the covering CSM in Account context before reaching out.
  7. Always leave with a follow-up task — converted-but-untouched is how leads die.
  8. "Convert blocked" means a data-quality check stopped it — read the message; nothing fails silently.

7. What gets recorded (for RevOps)

Every convert stamps telemetry on the Lead: Conversion_Source__c = "Smart Convert", Match_Confidence__c (ONECLICK / REVIEW / GUIDED_CREATE), Opportunity_Motion__c, New_Account_Created__c, Recommended_Office__c, Overrode_Recommendation__c, Manual_Search_Used__c, Create_Reason__c, Disqualify_Reason__c. This powers duplicate-creation, motion-mix and matcher-feedback reporting, and shows where matching needs enrichment.

Reporting caveat: Opportunity_Motion__c on leads converted before 24 September 2026 over-states Cross-sell (1,152 of the first 2,231 conversions were stamped Cross-sell under the old rule). Salesforce locks converted leads, so that history can't be corrected. For motion reporting use Opportunity Type — the ten open opportunities still carrying the old "Repeat Business" were corrected on 24 September.

Appendix — environment notes

  • Each convert consumes its lead — use a fresh lead per test.
  • Prefer real inbound leads over hand-made test leads — hand-made leads carry no Pardot score, no email-verification status and often no address, so several panels legitimately show less.
  • Sandbox email deliverability is usually restricted — Data Team notification emails may not deliver from sandboxes.
  • Office cards compute their numbers live when stored rollups are unpopulated, so figures always reflect actual opportunity data.
  • For T24, the tester's own User → Team decides the fallback — check yours before running it.
