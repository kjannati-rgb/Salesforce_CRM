If you read nothing else

The 30-second version

  1. Open the Lead → click Smart Convert. If a green card appears and the details look right, Convert now — you're done in one click.
  2. Location decides the office, not deal counts — the office's address becomes the contact's address.
  3. If the email matches someone we know, the wizard links to them. Never create a second copy of a person.
  4. Already subscribes to this product, or a related one? The wizard makes it a Renewal, Upsell or Cross-sell for you. A client of other, unrelated products is still New Business for yours.
  5. Open deal already covers this product? Add to it — don't split the deal. (The opportunity toggle is off by default; switch it on only when this enquiry should open a new deal.)
  6. Check Account context for the CSM who covers this product before you reach out.
  7. Finish with the follow-up task — a converted lead with no next action goes cold.
  8. "Convert blocked" is the system explaining itself, not crashing. Read the message; it names the fix.

The clever part

Customer or prospect? The wizard already knows

Before you confirm, the system checks whether the firm actively subscribes to the product the enquiry is about — the one the lead names or, when it names none, your team's product line (the banner tells you when it's done that). Deals the firm has won on other, unrelated products don't count. What it finds changes what gets created:

Product families: Law.com — including Radar and Compass, plus VerdictSearch — and the Specialist Platforms titles (GAR, GCR, GIR, WTR, IAM and the rest of that group). Every Lexology product stands alone: a Lexology PRO subscription doesn't make them a Lexology Intelligence customer.

When it's not the happy path

Special situations

Several firms share the domain

You'll be asked to pick. Take the extra ten seconds — your choice decides where the contact and any deal live, and a wrong pick means Data Team cleanup. Each card shows the firm's domain, its office locations (so similarly named firms separate at a glance) and its footprint (offices · contacts · won deals) — compare against the lead's email; if unsure, check the firm's website first.

No match at all — create instantly, sell faster

This used to be the slow lane: account creation was locked down, so a genuinely new company meant emailing the Data Team and waiting before you could even convert. Not any more. The wizard runs its duplicate checks, then builds the new firm and its first office on the spot — you pick one reason from a picklist and you're converting within seconds, not days. The office takes the company's name exactly as it appears on the lead, with the full address including state, so quotes and contracts show the right legal name. The Data Team is notified automatically to review and enrich the new account behind you, so speed and data quality both win.

Not a fit? Disqualify — but keep the person

The Disqualify button converts without an opportunity and records your reason. The person still lands under the right firm — because today's "not interested" is next year's renewal contact. Nothing is thrown away.

Do I still use the standard Convert button?
No — it's gone: the native Convert button has been removed from the Lead page. Smart Convert is how leads are converted: same outcome, minus the duplicates and the guesswork.
What if the wizard picks the wrong firm?
Use "Wrong firm? Search & pick a different one" on the office screen — search by firm or office name. Your correction is recorded and helps us improve the matching.
Why is the opportunity toggle off by default?
Not every enquiry should open a deal, and half of them belong on a deal that already exists. Switch it on when this enquiry genuinely starts something new.
The firm is already our client — why does it say New Business?
Because they don't subscribe to this product or a related one. Being a Lexology or Events client doesn't make a Law.com enquiry a cross-sell — for Law.com it's new business, and that's how it's reported.
