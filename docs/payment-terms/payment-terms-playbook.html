2What go-live changes — and what it doesn’t

Three approval mechanisms exist today. Flip the switch to see each one’s fate. Nothing changes in production until the sign-off gate.

3What happens to approvals already out there

Quotes already approved

Nothing changes retrospectively. Approved quotes keep their approved status and their full approval history. Rules only run at the moment a quote is submitted, so nothing is re-opened or re-evaluated.

Requests sitting with an approver at cutover

They simply complete under the rules they were submitted with — approve or reject as normal. The new chain attaches only to submissions made after go-live.

A rep recalls an old quote and resubmits

Resubmission is a fresh evaluation, so it routes through the new chain. One nuance: older quotes may not have a justification written — the rule demands one only when terms change, so early requests can arrive without it. Finance can simply ask in the approval comments.

Renewal quotes carrying legacy terms

Renewals created automatically with old terms (say, Net 45 from a 2024 contract) are never blocked at creation — the checks engage only when someone changes terms or submits for approval. Renewal processing is not interrupted; Finance approval still happens on submission.

4Decisions and open points

60 vs 120 days — decided: extended list stays

Decided: the extended list stays. The production terms list offers Net 75, Net 90 and Net 120 (unused for at least two years). Rather than retiring them, they remain available for the extremely rare cases the process document allows — policed by exactly the same rules as Net 45/60: blocked under the threshold, justification required, routed through the two-step chain. One correction for the document before cascade: soften the “60 days is the absolute maximum” guardrail line to match its own approvals table — terms above 60 days are possible in extremely rare circumstances, with the same Finance approval.

Net 30 routing for some teams — open question

The pre-existing generic Finance check currently also routes Net 15/30 quotes from most teams to Finance. We have deliberately left that untouched — it is existing behaviour, not part of this change — but it sits oddly against the policy line that 30 days or less is self-service. This is the one open question for Finance: keep that routing, or align with the policy and make 30 days or less fully self-service? Either is a small change once decided.

Exchange-rate constant — for awareness

The $10,000 line is applied as GBP 7,500. At today’s USD rate that admits quotes from about $9,500 — slightly generous. Worth revisiting if FX moves materially.

Requiring a PO — two levels, both Credit Control’s

Per customer: the “PO Required” flag the invoicing team maintains on the account (around 270 customers marked Yes today) will make the PO automatically mandatory on the order form — no signer choice. Ships with the order form release. Per deal: when approving extended terms, Credit Control can set “PO Required” to Yes on that specific quote as a condition — try the checkbox in the approval walkthrough above. Either way, the client cannot complete signing without entering the PO number, and it syncs straight back to the quote.
